# Creative Direction & Best-in-Class Design Standards
<!-- id: creative.root -->

Purpose: Raise websites, apps, SaaS products, portals, marketplaces, dashboards, and digital brands from competent template quality to distinctive, premium, production-ready design.

This module complements `DESIGN.md`.

`DESIGN.md` determines industry-appropriate design patterns and suitable implementation libraries.

`CREATIVE.md` determines how the work should be art-directed, differentiated, visually refined, reviewed, and improved.

---

# Creative Quality Principle
<!-- id: creative.quality-principle -->

The goal is not merely to make something look modern.

The result SHOULD feel:

- specific to the brand
- appropriate to the industry
- intentional
- coherent
- memorable
- useful
- credible
- accessible
- performant
- finished

Visual design MUST reinforce comprehension, trust, task completion, conversion, or product usability.

Decoration without purpose is not design quality.

---

# Best-in-Class Workflow
<!-- id: creative.best-in-class-workflow -->

For substantial visual work:

```text
Understand
↓
Resolve Industry + Audience + Goal
↓
Inspect Current Category Context When Research Is Available
↓
Define Brand / Product Character
↓
Explore Multiple Creative Directions
↓
Select One Direction
↓
Define Visual System
↓
Generate / Prepare Required Local Assets
↓
Design Key Screens and States
↓
Implement Responsively
↓
Render Real Output
↓
Inspect Desktop + Mobile + Critical States
↓
Refine
↓
Validate
↓
Deliver
```

Do not begin by assembling random components.

# Competitive Context Standard
<!-- id: creative.competitive-context -->

For production-intent public-facing visual work, when browsing or research tools are available, MUST inspect several current high-quality category references before finalizing art direction. For non-production or narrow internal UI work, this remains a SHOULD when it materially improves the result.

Research MAY include:

- category leaders
- respected digital products
- strong editorial experiences
- high-quality industry specialists
- relevant award-winning work
- adjacent industries with similar trust or interaction requirements

The purpose is to understand:

- current category conventions
- user expectations
- quality baseline
- overused patterns
- differentiation opportunities

MUST NOT copy another site's proprietary composition, copy, imagery, or visual identity.

Use references to establish a quality bar, not to imitate.

---

# Concept Exploration Standard
<!-- id: creative.concept-exploration -->

Before committing to a major visual direction for substantial production-intent visual work, MUST consider at least three materially different concepts internally. The user does not need to see all concepts unless requested; the purpose is to prevent the first plausible template from becoming the final design.

Concepts SHOULD differ in more than accent color.

Vary:

- page composition
- typography character
- density
- image treatment
- geometry
- navigation
- section rhythm
- content hierarchy
- motion character
- brand personality

Example:

```text
Direction A
Editorial / quiet / premium

Direction B
Technical / structured / information-rich

Direction C
Human / warm / image-led
```

If the user requests multiple designs, differences MUST be structural and strategic rather than recolored duplicates.

# Art Direction Definition
<!-- id: creative.art-direction-definition -->

A substantial visual creation SHOULD define:

- visual personality
- emotional tone
- typography character
- image style
- color behavior
- shape language
- spacing rhythm
- motion character
- icon style
- illustration style when used
- density level
- content hierarchy
- interaction personality

These decisions SHOULD reinforce one another.

# Brand Specificity Standard
<!-- id: creative.brand-specificity -->

Do not create a design that could belong to any company in the industry.

Meaningful visual decisions SHOULD derive from:

- positioning
- target audience
- service/product model
- customer expectations
- trust requirements
- brand promise
- differentiators
- geography when relevant
- content type
- business maturity

# Anti-Generic AI Design Guard
<!-- id: creative.anti-generic-ai -->

Avoid habitual AI/template aesthetics unless the project context specifically justifies them.

Common weak patterns:

- arbitrary purple/blue gradients
- glowing decorative blobs
- excessive glassmorphism
- every section inside rounded cards
- repetitive three-card feature rows
- excessive pill buttons
- fake floating dashboard widgets
- meaningless "AI" particle effects
- decorative grids with no information purpose
- identical section spacing from top to bottom
- generic startup illustrations
- generic smiling stock-photo hero compositions
- decorative charts with fabricated values
- giant empty hero areas
- trendy typography that harms readability
- excessive animation used as visual compensation
- repeated icon + title + two-line-copy cards across an entire page

Use these only when they genuinely fit the brand and information architecture.

---

# Layout Composition Standard
<!-- id: creative.layout-composition -->

Premium layouts SHOULD create deliberate rhythm.

Use variation in:

- width
- alignment
- negative space
- density
- media scale
- typography scale
- grouping
- foreground/background relationships

Avoid making every section:

```text
Centered heading
↓
Centered paragraph
↓
Three equal cards
```

A page SHOULD have recognizable moments and pacing.

---

# Hero Standard
<!-- id: creative.hero -->

A hero SHOULD immediately communicate:

- what the organization/product is
- who it is for
- why it matters
- the primary next action

The hero MAY be:

- editorial
- image-led
- product-led
- data-led
- interaction-led
- typography-led

Do not automatically use a split-screen hero.

Do not use decorative media that competes with comprehension.

---

# Typography Standard
<!-- id: creative.typography -->

Typography is a primary design material.

A strong system SHOULD define:

- display scale
- heading scale
- body scale
- labels
- captions
- numeric/data styles
- line height
- measure
- weight hierarchy
- emphasis behavior

Prefer a small number of intentional type styles over dozens of arbitrary sizes.

Body copy MUST remain comfortably readable.

Typography SHOULD carry hierarchy even before color, borders, or cards are added.

---

# Spacing & Rhythm Standard
<!-- id: creative.spacing-rhythm -->

Use a coherent spacing system.

Spacing SHOULD communicate:

- grouping
- hierarchy
- section transitions
- component relationships

Avoid uniform vertical gaps that flatten the page.

Dense applications and editorial marketing pages may require different rhythm while still sharing tokens.

---

# Color Standard
<!-- id: creative.color -->

Color SHOULD have semantic and brand roles.

Define:

- primary brand color
- secondary/supporting color
- surfaces
- text colors
- borders
- interactive states
- success/warning/error/info
- data visualization palette if required

Avoid using color simply to make empty compositions feel interesting.

Accessibility contrast requirements override aesthetic preference.

---

# Image Art Direction Standard
<!-- id: creative.image-art-direction -->

Imagery SHOULD have a defined art direction.

Specify as appropriate:

- subject
- environment
- camera perspective
- crop behavior
- lighting
- depth
- texture
- color temperature
- realism/stylization
- human presence
- background complexity
- negative space for text
- brand relationship

Images across one experience SHOULD feel like they belong to the same visual system.

Use `ASSETS.md` for creation, storage, optimization, and integration rules.

---

# Iconography Standard
<!-- id: creative.iconography -->

Use one primary icon language unless there is a deliberate reason to mix systems.

Icons SHOULD have consistent:

- stroke/fill treatment
- optical size
- corner character
- visual weight
- alignment

Do not use icons where plain language is clearer.

---

# Illustration Standard
<!-- id: creative.illustration -->

Illustration SHOULD be art-directed rather than used as filler.

It MAY support:

- explanations
- onboarding
- abstract concepts
- empty states
- branded storytelling

Avoid generic vector illustrations that could belong to unrelated products.

---

# Motion Standard
<!-- id: creative.motion -->

Motion SHOULD explain relationships, state changes, hierarchy, or brand character.

Prefer:

- subtle entrance choreography
- state transitions
- progressive disclosure
- meaningful hover/focus response
- spatial continuity

Avoid:

- scroll-jacking
- long blocking intros
- constant floating elements
- gratuitous parallax
- animation on every card
- motion that delays task completion

Respect reduced-motion preferences.

---

# Interaction Craft Standard
<!-- id: creative.interaction-craft -->

Interactive elements SHOULD feel deliberate.

Check:

- hover
- focus
- pressed
- selected
- disabled
- loading
- success
- error
- empty
- destructive
- permission denied
- offline where relevant

A polished screen is incomplete if only its default state is designed.

---

# Responsive Art Direction Standard
<!-- id: creative.responsive-art-direction -->

Mobile MUST be intentionally composed.

Do not merely stack desktop sections vertically.

Review:

- priority changes
- navigation
- tap targets
- reading order
- image crops
- sticky actions
- dense tables
- filters
- forms
- modals
- media
- chart behavior

Desktop and mobile SHOULD feel like the same product, not one layout compressed.

---

# Content Realism Standard
<!-- id: creative.content-realism -->

Do not use `Lorem ipsum` in production-intent work.

Do not fabricate:

- awards
- customer counts
- certifications
- ratings
- testimonials
- clinical outcomes
- legal claims
- security guarantees
- company history

When business facts are unknown, use truthful neutral copy or clearly configurable content fields.

Design with realistic content lengths.

---

# Data Visualization Integrity
<!-- id: creative.data-visualization-integrity -->

Charts and dashboards MUST represent meaningful information architecture.

Do not invent impressive-looking numbers merely to decorate a production artifact.

For demonstration data explicitly requested by the user, label or isolate it as demo/sample data.

Use chart types that fit the question being answered.

---

# Navigation Standard
<!-- id: creative.navigation -->

Navigation SHOULD reflect real information architecture.

Avoid hiding essential destinations merely for visual minimalism.

Navigation SHOULD make:

- location
- available actions
- hierarchy
- current state

understandable.

---

# Conversion Design Standard
<!-- id: creative.conversion -->

Conversion design SHOULD reduce uncertainty rather than merely increase CTA frequency.

Use:

- clear value proposition
- evidence
- risk reduction
- transparent next steps
- appropriate calls to action
- pricing/eligibility clarity
- helpful objections handling

Do not use deceptive urgency or dark patterns.

---

# Trust Design Standard
<!-- id: creative.trust -->

Trust-critical industries SHOULD prioritize:

- transparent identity
- qualifications
- process clarity
- contact information
- policies
- evidence
- security/privacy explanation where relevant
- realistic imagery
- accessible communication

Do not substitute luxury styling for credibility.

---

# Accessibility Quality Standard
<!-- id: creative.accessibility -->

For public web and application work, SHOULD target WCAG 2.2 Level AA unless the project requires another defined target.

Visual review MUST include:

- text contrast
- focus visibility
- keyboard operation
- zoom/reflow
- target sizing
- labels
- alternative text
- reduced motion
- meaningful heading order
- non-color-only status communication

Official reference:
https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/

---

# Performance Quality Standard
<!-- id: creative.performance -->

Premium visual design MUST remain performant.

For public web experiences, use current Core Web Vitals guidance as an engineering quality target.

At the time of this pack version, the recommended "good" thresholds are:

- LCP: 2.5 seconds or less
- INP: 200 milliseconds or less
- CLS: 0.1 or less

Evaluate at the 75th percentile where field data is available.

Official reference:
https://web.dev/articles/vitals

Do not ship massive imagery, animation, 3D, video, or JavaScript simply to appear premium.

---

# Asset Budget Standard
<!-- id: creative.asset-budget -->

Visual richness SHOULD have a budget.

Before adding heavy media, ask:

- Does it improve understanding?
- Does it improve differentiation?
- Does it improve trust?
- Does it materially support conversion?
- Can it be lazy-loaded?
- Can it be compressed?
- Can a simpler asset achieve the same result?

---

# Real Render Review
<!-- id: creative.real-render-review -->

When implementation/rendering tools are available, substantial visual work SHOULD be inspected as an actual rendered experience before delivery.

Review at minimum:

- desktop viewport
- mobile viewport
- top of page
- middle content
- bottom/footer
- navigation state
- primary form/action
- one error/empty/loading state when applicable

Do not rely solely on source code or design intent.

---

# Screenshot Refinement Loop
<!-- id: creative.screenshot-refinement-loop -->

When screenshot/render tools are available:

```text
Render
↓
Inspect
↓
Identify visual defects
↓
Correct
↓
Render again
```

Check:

- accidental whitespace
- cramped content
- alignment
- text wrapping
- awkward crops
- visual balance
- excessive repetition
- contrast
- button hierarchy
- responsive issues
- broken overflow
- inconsistent radii
- icon alignment
- footer quality

Repeat until no material visual issue remains.

# Production Visual Acceptance Gate
<!-- id: creative.production-visual-acceptance-gate -->

This gate is mandatory for production-intent public-facing websites, landing pages, brand sites, and substantial marketing frontends. Technical completeness does not substitute for visual quality.

Before delivery, the work MUST satisfy all applicable checks below.

## 1. Category Benchmark Gate

When current research tools are available:

- inspect at least 3 strong current category references;
- inspect at least 1 adjacent-category reference when it offers a useful composition, typography, editorial, or interaction benchmark;
- identify conventions to preserve, overused patterns to avoid, and at least 2 differentiation opportunities;
- use references as a quality bar only; MUST NOT copy proprietary composition, copy, imagery, or identity.

If research tools are unavailable, use established category knowledge but do not claim current-market benchmarking was completed.

## 2. Concept Selection Gate

Consider at least 3 materially different directions before implementation. Directions MUST differ structurally, not merely by color. Select one based on the organization's audience, offer, trust model, conversion goal, content, and category expectations.

The selected direction MUST define at least:

- composition strategy;
- typography character;
- image/media treatment;
- spacing/density rhythm;
- shape/surface language;
- interaction/motion character;
- conversion emphasis.

## 3. Project-Specific Design Fingerprint

A production design MUST contain at least 3 meaningful visual or interaction decisions that derive from this exact business, product, audience, geography, offer, proof model, or content.

If the company name and logo could be replaced with a competitor's without materially changing the experience, refinement is required.

## 4. Composition Repetition Gate

For a medium or long marketing page:

- use at least 3 materially different section compositions before the footer when content supports them;
- MUST NOT repeat the same centered-heading + paragraph + equal-card grammar across most of the page;
- MUST NOT use more than 2 consecutive sections with essentially the same grid/card structure unless the content demands it;
- create at least one memorable high-value moment beyond the hero, such as an editorial proof section, immersive service story, before/after treatment, project gallery, process composition, map/service-area moment, or distinctive conversion section.

## 5. Hero Quality Gate

The first viewport MUST establish a clear hierarchy and a deliberate brand impression.

Fail the hero when it is primarily:

- a generic split-screen composition with interchangeable copy and media;
- oversized empty space without useful visual tension;
- a low-fidelity decorative illustration standing in for proof-bearing imagery;
- a stock-template pattern with no brand-specific composition decision;
- multiple competing CTAs with no clear primary action.

## 6. Typography & Spacing Gate

Typography and spacing MUST create hierarchy before decorative effects are added.

- Display, heading, body, label, and caption roles must be visibly distinct and coherent.
- Body measure and line-height must support comfortable reading.
- Section pacing must vary with content importance rather than using one repeated vertical padding value everywhere.
- Premium marketing work SHOULD avoid relying on an unmodified browser/default system stack as its only brand expression when a stronger accessible type treatment is feasible.

## 7. Media Fidelity Gate

Key media MUST match the category's proof model and the intended brand quality.

- Reality-dependent categories such as landscaping, construction, real estate, hospitality, food, automotive, beauty, home services, travel, and physical products SHOULD prioritize high-fidelity contextual or photographic-style media when imagery materially supports trust.
- Low-detail generic vector art MUST NOT be used as a substitute merely because it is easy to generate locally.
- Generated imagery MUST NOT be presented as verified client work, completed projects, awards, or testimonials unless it is factual source material.
- Crops, focal points, aspect ratios, and mobile behavior must be intentionally designed.

Use `ASSETS.md` for generation and truth-safe integration.

## 8. Responsive Recomposition Gate

Mobile MUST be re-composed, not merely stacked. Verify navigation, hero crop, type scale, section order, touch targets, gallery behavior, forms, and sticky actions. At least one meaningful priority or composition adaptation SHOULD occur where the desktop structure would otherwise feel compressed.

## 9. Render-Based Refinement Gate

When rendering/screenshot tooling exists, MUST:

1. render representative desktop and mobile views;
2. score and inspect the actual pixels, not only source code;
3. correct material weaknesses;
4. re-render affected views;
5. repeat until the quality threshold and critical-failure checks pass.

Source validity, clean code, and responsive CSS are necessary but are not evidence that the design looks premium.

## 10. Genericity Rejection Test

Before finalizing, ask internally:

- Does this look like a recognizable category-leading experience rather than a starter template?
- Is the visual hierarchy obvious within seconds?
- Is there a coherent image strategy rather than filler?
- Are there memorable moments beyond color choice?
- Does the design visibly reflect the exact organization and conversion task?

A material "no" to any of these requires another refinement pass.

# Visual Quality Threshold
<!-- id: creative.quality-threshold -->

For production-intent public-facing visual work, this is an acceptance gate rather than a suggestion.

```text
92–100
Production acceptance range / best-in-class target

85–91
Strong professional quality, but refinement required before a premium production delivery

75–84
Competent but visibly under-refined

Below 75
Rework required
```

Production acceptance also requires these category floors when applicable:

- Art Direction: at least 13/15
- Composition & Hierarchy: at least 13/15
- Typography: at least 8/10
- Imagery / Iconography: at least 8/10 when media is material
- Interaction & Responsive Craft: at least 8/10
- Content & Trust: at least 8/10
- Accessibility: at least 9/10

Critical failures override numerical score. A score MUST NOT be used to excuse a generic or template-like result. When rendering tools are available, score the rendered result rather than inferred source quality.

# Critical Visual Failures
<!-- id: creative.critical-failures -->

Do not finalize with:

- broken responsive layout
- unreadable text
- inaccessible primary controls
- generic placeholder imagery
- fake trust claims
- obvious repeated template sections
- dead primary CTA
- major visual inconsistency
- missing required states
- clipped content
- horizontal mobile overflow
- severe layout shift caused by media
- imagery that contradicts the business or audience
- obvious "AI-generated template" aesthetics with no project-specific art direction

# Final Creative Standard
<!-- id: creative.final-standard -->

The result should feel designed for this exact organization, audience, and task.

A premium digital experience is not defined by how many visual effects it contains.

It is defined by the quality of its decisions.
